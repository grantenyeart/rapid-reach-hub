Calling to sell AI

This is the call where AI is the pitch from the first sentence. You are not asking about ceramics and you are not fishing for which product fits. You picked it before you dialled.

The framework, every product

Opener → hook → question. The opener is the same on every call. The hook is the one question that exposes the problem this product solves. The question makes them say the problem out loud. Then you pitch.

1Opener

Say this

"Hey, is this [Company]?"

let them say yes

"Okay, perfect. I was actually calling to see, what time do you guys close at?"

Why it is this and not the ceramics question

The ceramics question exists to work out which product to sell. You already know. So you skip it and spend your opener on the hook instead. Asking their closing time is harmless, everybody answers it, and it sets the trap in one line.

2Hook

They say a time. Say this

they say "we close at 6"

"Okay, so if I gave you a call at 7pm, would you guys answer that, or would I just have to leave a voicemail?"

This is the whole call

You are not asking for information. You are getting them to say the problem out loud in their own words. An owner who tells you "yeah, you would just leave a voicemail" has just admitted he loses calls, and he cannot un-admit it.

Do not rush this. Let them finish the sentence.

3The turn

Say this

they say "just leave a voicemail, we'll get back to you"

"Well John, that's exactly why I called.

62% of customers won't call a business back if nobody picks up the first time, and only 43% even leave a voicemail. So most of what hits that voicemail box was already gone.

I've got a receptionist that answers every one of those for you, in your voice, and books them straight into your calendar."

Use the number

You know these from the training. 62% will not call back. Only 43% leave a voicemail. The stat is what turns "I'll get back to them" into "I am losing jobs." Say it right after they admit the voicemail, not before.

4Question, straight into discovery

Now you are in the discovery you already know. Same questions as the main module, in this order.

Work through these

"How many phone calls a day do you think you're getting?"

"Are you the one answering the phone and doing the details? One hand on the phone, one hand on the vacuum?"

"Is it loud when a customer's trying to talk to you?"

"How many missed calls a day do you think you're getting? And what do you do with those, get back to them the next day?"

"How much are you paying a receptionist right now?"

No number, no diagnosis

You need their call volume and their missed-call count before you pitch anything. Without a number you have nothing to do maths on, and the maths is what closes this.

5Say the money back to them

Say this

"So John, you're out there doing the details and answering the phone, and you're missing about two calls a day. Your average interior and exterior detail is around $300.

That's $600 in opportunity walking out every single day, before we even talk about the ones where you're on the phone with a vacuum running in the background.

That's exactly why I called. Let's hop on a quick call so you can hear it, and I'll build you a demo in your own voice before we talk."

The demo is the close

Offering to build a demo in their voice before the Zoom is the strongest thing on this call. Nobody turns down hearing their own voice answer a phone. Use it every time.

6Objections

"AI can't sell to my customers"
"Have you ever called a dentist or an orthodontist? That was an AI receptionist and you didn't notice. Let's get you on a call so you can hear ours."
"I don't want AI in my business"
"Totally fair. The question isn't AI or no AI, it's whether that 7pm call gets answered or goes to the guy down the road. We can also set it so it only catches the ones you miss."
"I don't miss that many calls"
"That's great, and easy to check. What happens to the ones after you close? Even two a day at $300 is $600."
"How much is it?"
"Our pricing and packages vary based on what you're looking for, and that's exactly what the call is for."
The rule

You do not quote a price on a setting call. Pricing lives on the Zoom. Your job is to get them there.

7Book the Zoom

Say this

"I'll set up a quick call with one of our specialists, they'll play it for you and show you exactly how it books into your calendar. Do you have any availability today?"

  1. Push same day first. If no, tomorrow.
  2. Pin a specific time. "Morning or evening?" then "What about 1pm?"
  3. Email, send the link now, give your number. "Text me if anything comes up."
  4. Put the booking in the bookings tab in Discord straight after the call.
