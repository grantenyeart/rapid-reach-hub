Rapid Reach · Closer Training

Killing No-Shows
On Your Side

The setter earned you the call. From the second it hits your calendar it is yours, and it stays yours even after they do not turn up.

Start here

A no-show is not a dead lead.
It is a lead you have not reached yet.

Most closers treat 2:05 as the end of the call. They sit on the Zoom for ten minutes, close the tab and mark it no-show. That booking cost a setter days of dialing, and it just got written off in ten minutes of silence.

The job

Your job is not to attend the call. It is to make the call happen. Everything here is either making them want to be there before the time, or going and getting them after it.

The process

Five moves, every booking

1
The second it books, send a selfie and a text
Before the day of. The moment it lands on your calendar.
2
Five minutes past, call them
Do not sit on the Zoom waiting. Pick up the phone.
3
No answer, call again
Always twice. Never once.
4
Still no answer, leave a voicemail
Every time, no exceptions.
5
Then the text that re-books it
The audit is built. That is your reason to be in their phone.

One happens before. Two through five all happen inside about four minutes of each other.

Move 1 · The second it books

Send a selfie, then the text

Not on the day. The moment the booking hits your calendar. Take a normal photo of yourself, nothing staged, and send it with this.

Word for word
Hey John, see that we have a scheduled call. Doing some research on your company and your competition now. Looking forward to our call. Speak here soon
Why the selfie, and not just a text

They booked a call with a company. Now they have a face. It is a lot harder to blow off a person than a calendar invite, and a photo gets opened when a block of text gets ignored.

Then the words do the second job. "Doing some research on your company and your competition now" tells them work has already started on their behalf. Not showing up now costs somebody effort, and people do not like that.

Move 2 · Five minutes past

Do not sit on the Zoom. Call them.

Five minutes past the start time, pick up the phone. Not at ten past, not at fifteen. Five.

Word for word
Hey John, I'm hopping on our call now to go over your free audit. Is now still a good time for you?
Read what that line is doing

It assumes the call is happening. You are not asking whether they forgot or whether they still want to, you are telling them you are on it right now and checking the timing.

And "your free audit" is in there on purpose. It is already built, it is theirs, and it is sitting there. That is a very different call to receive than "hey, you missed our meeting."

Move 3 · No answer

Always call twice

One missed call is a stranger. Two in a row is somebody who actually needs to reach you, and that is when people pick up.

Think about your own phone

You ignore one unknown number without a thought. When the same number rings straight back, you look at it. Nothing else about you changed. Only that you called twice.

Most closers call once, get nothing, and decide the person is dodging them. They were in a driveway with wet hands.

Move 4 · Still nothing

Leave the voicemail. Every time.

Short, warm, and about the audit, not about them missing it.

Roughly this
Hey John, Grant with Rapid Reach. Just hopping on our call and I have got your audit built out here. Give me a ring back when you get a sec and we will get through it.
Why it is not optional

If you will not leave a message, you are telling them the call was not worth one. A missed ring is nothing. A voice they recognise is a reason to pick up next time.

Never make them feel caught out. No "you missed our call." Nobody books a second meeting with someone who made them feel bad about the first.

Move 5 · Then re-book it

The text that gets it back

Word for word
Hey John, was looking forward to our call. I have your free audit built out. When are you available for 15 minutes to go over this?
Three things that line does

"Was looking forward to our call." Disappointed, not annoyed. They owe you nothing and you are not making them feel like they do.

"I have your free audit built out." The work exists. It is finished, it is theirs, and right now it is going to waste.

"When are you available for 15 minutes?" Not thirty. Fifteen is easy to say yes to, and the call runs as long as it runs once you are on it.

After that

Then you keep going

Move five is not the end of it. It is the first attempt at getting it back.

The standard

Same as it is for the setters. You chase it until they show or until they tell you no. Not until it feels awkward. Not until the fourth attempt.

A booked call that no-showed is still the warmest lead you have all week. They raised their hand, they gave a setter their problem, and they agreed to a time. None of that stopped being true because they were busy at two o'clock.

The whole thing

Every booking, every time

  • Selfie and text sent the moment it booked, not on the day
  • That text said I am already researching their company and their competition
  • At five minutes past I called, I did not sit on the Zoom
  • I asked "is now still a good time", not "did you forget"
  • I called a second time
  • I left a voicemail, and it was about the audit, not about them missing it
  • I sent the re-book text with "I have your free audit built out"
  • I will keep going until they show or until they tell me no
The one line

Two o'clock is not
the deadline.

It is just the time you agreed on. The lead is still warm at 2:05, at 2:30, and tomorrow morning. The only closers who lose those calls are the ones who decided they were lost.

Go get them.
