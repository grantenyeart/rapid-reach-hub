Killing No-Shows

The booked call is not the win. The call they actually turn up to is the win. There are two tracks here because setters and closers have completely different jobs in this. Do yours.

The six steps
  1. Earn the show before you book it. Pain, need, decision maker.
  2. Book a consultation, not a sales call. They turn up for a free audit.
  3. Ask the zombie apocalypse question. Make them say it out loud.
  4. Get the reply YES before you hang up. Watch them send it.
  5. Run the confirmation sequence. All of it, not the comfortable parts.
  6. Be unreasonable about the follow up. Until they show or they tell you no.

The five moves
  1. The second it books, selfie and text. Not on the day.
  2. Five minutes past, call them. Do not sit on the Zoom.
  3. No answer, call again. Always twice.
  4. Still nothing, leave a voicemail. Every time.
  5. Then the text that re-books it. The audit is already built.

After they do not show

Everyone

The call that gets it back, start to finish, real and unedited. This one had gone fourteen days cold and he still re-booked it inside three minutes.

Grant's own warning in the first ten seconds: it should never get two weeks out. The five moves above are how you stop it getting here. This is what it looks like when it does anyway.

What he does, in order · tap a time to jump there
  1. 0:45No apology, no guilt. "Does that ring a bell at all?" He never mentions that they missed it, never says sorry, never asks what happened. Two weeks of silence gets treated like it never happened, because making them explain themselves is how you lose the call a second time.
  2. 0:56Re-confirm the need before you re-pitch anything. "You can take on about 10 more details a week, correct?" He makes Dennis say the number out loud again. The reason to meet has to be alive before you go asking for a time.
  3. 1:05The reason to show up, and it is already built. "I actually built you out a free audit on your company." The work is done whether he turns up or not. That turns the call from a pitch into a handover, and it is the strongest line in here.
  4. 1:31Day first, then you narrow it. Dennis says Wednesday, so he asks morning or evening rather than reading out slots. They pick the day, you pick the window, and the time ends up exact instead of vague.
  5. 1:52Light scarcity, then check the clock. "I got a lot of calls scheduled here, I want to make sure we get you in the calendar." Then "what time is it currently for you?" Never book across a timezone you have not actually checked.
  6. 2:21The reply YES, same as the setter track. "I'm about to send you a confirmation text. Could you just respond yes so that you can get booked into the calendar?" This is not optional on a re-book. Somebody who already no-showed once needs the thumb print on it.
  7. 2:35The zombie apocalypse question, word for word. "Is there going to be anything that you could possibly see getting in the way so that you wouldn't be able to do this call here?" Asked after the time is locked, never before.
  8. 2:50Personal number, and permission to move it. "If anything does come up, just be like hey Grant, could we push it back an hour." He hands over an easy out that is not a no-show. People ghost because rescheduling feels rude to them. Take that away and they text you instead of vanishing.

Why this one matters more than the rest of your training. Every other skill you have only pays off if the prospect is actually on the call. A booked call that no-shows is not half a win, it is zero, and it cost everything it took to get it.
