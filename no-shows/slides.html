Rapid Reach · Sales Training

Killing
No-Shows

The booked call is not the win. The call they actually turn up to is the win. Everything in here is about closing the gap between those two.

Start here

Nobody forgets a call
they actually want to be on

A no-show is almost never a calendar problem. They did not lose the invite and they did not get confused about the time. Somewhere between your call and that morning, they decided half an hour with us was not worth it.

Which changes where the work is

The show is not won in the reminder. It is won on the setting call. Reminders only collect a decision that has already been made. Everything that follows is either building that decision or protecting it.

Step 1 · On the setting call

Earn the show before you book it

Three things have to be true before a call is worth putting on a closer's calendar. If you cannot answer all three, you have not booked a call. You have booked a no-show.

1
Did they tell you the pain, in their own words?
Not "yeah, I could use more work." The actual problem. Slow weeks, an empty calendar, an agency that burned them, a van sitting idle. If you cannot repeat their pain back to the closer, they have not told you any.
2
Do they actually need what we sell?
Booked out six weeks and happy is not a buyer. Someone who wants more work and is not getting it is.
3
Are you talking to the decision maker?
If they have to run it past a partner or an owner, that call will not close, and most of the time it will not even happen. Get the decision maker on, or get them both on.
The point

A prospect who has just described their own problem out loud has a reason to show up. One who agreed to a call to get you off the phone does not.

Step 2 · On the setting call

Book a consultation,
not a sales call

Nobody blocks out half an hour to be sold to. They will block out half an hour to find out what is wrong with their business.

Every call you set is a free consultation and a free audit. On it we go through:

●
Their Google Business Profile
What is set up wrong on it, and what that is costing them.
●
Their SEO and where they actually rank
Who is sitting above them in their own town, and why.
●
How much work is slipping past them right now
The searches happening in their area that are going to somebody else.
Say it out loud before you hang up

They keep the audit whether they buy or not. Tell them that. A free teardown of their own business is worth showing up for. A pitch is not.

Step 3 · Before you hang up

Ask the question that makes them commit

Word for word
Other than a zombie apocalypse, is there any other reason you would not be able to make this call?
Why it works

It is light enough that they laugh, and specific enough that they have to answer.

Either you get a clean "no, I will be there", which is now a promise they would have to break to your face. Or they surface the real conflict and you move the call to a time they can actually make.

Both of those beat finding out at 2pm tomorrow.

Step 4 · Before you hang up

Get the reply YES while they are still on the phone

The moment they book, they get a text: two testimonial videos, where the call is, and a line asking them to reply YES to confirm.

Walk them to it
I just sent you a text. Can you open it and reply YES so I know the system has you locked in?
Do not hang up and hope

Wait for it. A confirmation you watched them send beats ten reminders you sent into silence, and a text they will not open while you are on the phone with them is telling you right now that this call is in trouble.

Step 5 · Between booking and call

The confirmation sequence

Run every one of these. Not the ones that feel comfortable. All of them.

On the call
Confirmation text sent, and replied YES, watched, before you hang up.
That morning
Text them. Short, warm, no pressure. "Looking forward to this afternoon."
That morning
Call them. Not instead of the text. As well as the text.
15 min before
Always. "Hey, looking forward to our call, just want to confirm you are still good to jump on."
No answer
Always leave a voicemail. A missed ring is nothing. A voice they recognise is a reason to pick up next time.
Step 6 · The standard

Be unreasonable about it

You dialed for days to get this person on the phone. You got them talking about the thing that keeps them up at night. You put it on a closer's calendar. Then you let it die because you did not want to seem pushy on the fourth attempt.

The standard
Fifteen calls in three days if that is what it takes.
How hard would you chase it

If somebody had a gun to your head, you would not be reasonable about following up. You would not stop at three.

This is the most valuable half hour anybody on this team produces all week. Chase it like it is.

The whole thing

Before any call counts as booked

  • They told me their pain, in their own words, and I can repeat it back
  • They actually need this, they are not booked out and happy
  • I am talking to the decision maker
  • I booked a free consultation and audit, not a sales call, and I said so
  • I asked the zombie apocalypse question and got a clean yes
  • I watched them reply YES before I hung up
  • Morning text, morning call, fifteen minutes before, voicemail every time
  • I will chase it until they show or until they tell me no
The one line

Booking the call is
the easy half.

Anyone on this team can get a yes on the phone. The job is getting them to still want it tomorrow afternoon, and that is decided by what you did before you hung up.
